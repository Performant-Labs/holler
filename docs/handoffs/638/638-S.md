## Quality audit

**Correctness and failure handling.**

- **Fail closed.** A standing fault answers every port method and every `next()` of an open watch, and it changes nothing (`fault.rs:119-133`). `enter` is the first call in each method. `a_corrupt_store_fails_closed_everywhere` covers `store-corrupt`, and the wedged tests cover `timeout`.
- **No lost writes or wakeups.**
  - **Writes.** Every read and write of the log happens under one lock.
  - **Watchers.** A watcher computes what it is owed under that lock and then waits with `Condvar::wait_timeout`, which releases the lock atomically. The poll loop recomputes after a spurious wakeup.
  - **Notify.** A writer notifies only after its guard is dropped (`feed.rs:165-174` and `:197-213`). The guard is a temporary of a `let` initializer, so lifetime extension does not keep it alive.
- **No lock-order inversion.** The fault switch's lock and the feed's lock are never held together. `enter` runs before the feed is touched, and the delay sleeps outside the switch's lock (`fault.rs:94-101`).
- **Overflow.** A generation overflow is `store-corrupt` through `next_generation`. A cursor overflow is `store-corrupt` before any mutation (`feed.rs:82-85`).
- **Poisoned locks.** They are taken over with `unwrap_or_else(PoisonError::into_inner)` (`fault.rs:106`, `feed.rs:210` and `:219`).

**Build guards.**

- **No panicking calls in `src/`.** There is no `unwrap`, `expect`, `panic!` or `assert!`. The grep hits only two lines inside the `text` doc fence at `conformance/pane_store.rs:96` and `:99`, which is never compiled.
